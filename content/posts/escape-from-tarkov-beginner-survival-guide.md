Escape from Tarkov is one of the most punishing and unforgiving shooters on the market.  
If you play it like a normal FPS, you will lose gear, rubles, and your sanity very quickly.

This guide is designed to help **new players survive their first week** in Tarkov by focusing on:
- Staying alive more often
- Extracting with loot consistently
- Avoiding the most common beginner mistakes

---

## 1. Mindset: Tarkov is Not a Run-and-Gun Shooter

The first thing you must accept:

> In Tarkov, **staying alive is more important than getting kills**.

Every death costs you:
- Your gun and armor (if not insured or not returned)
- Ammo, meds, backpacks, and loot
- Time and mental energy

So early on, your goal should be:
- **Win by extracting**, not by wiping the lobby  
- **Pick your fights**, instead of rushing every gunshot you hear

If you are out-geared or out-positioned, it is **perfectly fine to run away**.

---

## 2. Choose a Faction and Learn the Basic Settings

### BEAR vs USEC – Which Should You Pick?

For beginners, it honestly doesn’t matter too much, but:

- **USEC**
  - English voice lines
  - Better for playing with Western teammates
- **BEAR**
  - Russian voice lines
  - Some perks in certain patches (subject to balance changes)

Pick whatever you like, but **stick to one main PMC and learn its voice lines** so you don’t confuse teammates.

### Basic Settings to Adjust

Before jumping into raids, tweak these:

1. **FOV**  
   - Set it higher (around 65–75) so you see more around you.

2. **Mouse Sensitivity**  
   - Make sure hip-fire and ADS feel comfortable.
   - It’s better to be slightly too low than too high.

3. **Graphics**  
   - Lower shadows and post-processing to make enemies more visible.
   - Stable FPS is far more important than pretty graphics.

## 3. Start With SCAV Raids to Learn Maps Safely

As a beginner, your **SCAV is your best friend**.

### Why SCAV First?

- You spawn with **free random gear**.
- AI and players often ignore you at the start (depending on karma and behavior).
- If you extract successfully, you keep:
  - All the items you found
  - All the gear you spawned with

This lets you:
- Learn map layouts and extraction points **without risking your PMC gear**
- Build up early money, ammo, meds, and barter items

**Tip:** Try to extract with your SCAV as often as possible in your first few sessions.

---

## 4. Map Choice: Where Should Beginners Play?

Some good starter maps:

### Customs
- Great for early quests
- Medium difficulty
- Lots of PvP in certain hotspots (Dorms, gas station, construction)

### Woods
- More open and slower paced
- Good for learning ballistics and long sightlines
- Less hot than Customs in many areas

### Avoid as a Complete Beginner (at least at first)

- **Labs** – no insurance, very sweaty players
- **Streets of Tarkov** – complex, heavy on performance
- **Factory** – very small and very unforgiving, full of chads

## 5. Early PMC Loadouts: Cheap and Effective

When starting out, **don’t bring your “best” gear**.  
Focus on **budget but functional** kits:

### Weapon Tips

- Use whatever **low-recoil rifles or SMGs** you can afford:
  - Early AK variants
  - Budget AR-15s
  - MP5 / MPX / UMP-type guns
- Use **decent ammo before fancy attachments**.
  - In Tarkov, ammo > gun > armor > attachments.

### Armor and Helmet

- **Light armor** is fine at the start (Class 3–4).
- Helmets are optional early on:
  - They rarely save you from top-tier ammo.
  - But they can block fragmentation and low-tier rounds sometimes.

### Bring the Essentials

Always bring at least:
- 1x **medkit** (AI-2 early, then Salewa/IFAK)
- 1x **painkiller** (Analgin, Morphine, etc.)
- 1x **splint** or CMS for limb damage
- Enough **ammo** and at least **2 magazines**

## 6. How to Play Your Raids: A Simple Game Plan

Here’s a simple early-game loop you can follow:

1. **Spawn → Move away from high-traffic hotspots**
2. Hit a few **low-risk loot areas** (stashes, side rooms, hidden spots)
3. Avoid third-partying loud fights if:
   - You don’t know the terrain
   - You don’t have good armor or ammo
4. Watch the **raid time** and plan your path to extract.
5. If your bag is **half-full and valuable**, seriously consider leaving.
6. Extract successfully → Sell / sort loot → Repeat.

Think of each raid like a small business:
- **Profit > Ego**

---

## 7. Money Management: What to Keep and What to Sell

When your stash is tiny and your money is low, follow this rule:

> Keep things that help you survive raids. Sell the rest.

### Things Beginners Should Keep

- Meds (Salewa, IFAK, bandages, etc.)
- Ammo for your main guns
- Basic armor and helmets
- Tactical rigs and backpacks
- Task items (check a quest list if you’re not sure)

### Things You Can Usually Sell Early

- High-value barter items you don’t know how to use (for now)
- Extra guns you’re not planning to use soon
- Extra mods that you don’t have room to store

Don’t hoard everything.  
If your stash is full of random junk, you’ll be too scared to take anything into raids.

---

## 8. Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Running heavy gear every raid**
   - You will die and go broke fast.
2. **Always running straight toward gunshots**
   - Third-partying is powerful, but only if you know sightlines and flanks.
3. **Ignoring audio**
   - In Tarkov, **sound is information**. Stand still and listen often.
4. **Not insuring gear**
   - Early on, it’s usually worth insuring cheap weapons and armor.
5. **Chasing kills instead of loot**
   - Surviving with loot grows your account faster than hunting players.

---

## 9. Simple Checklist for Your First Week

Try to follow this checklist in your first 5–10 hours:

- [ ] Run multiple **SCAV raids** to learn a couple of maps  
- [ ] Unlock early traders and basic tasks  
- [ ] Set up 1–2 **budget PMC loadouts** you’re comfortable with  
- [ ] Successfully extract **at least 3–5 raids in a row** with your PMC  
- [ ] Learn **at least 2 extraction points** per map you play  
- [ ] Start recognizing common **hot zones** and avoid rushing them blindly  

If you can do that, you’re already ahead of most frustrated new players.

---

## Final Thoughts

Escape from Tarkov is meant to feel harsh and punishing at first.  
But once you understand that:

- Every raid is about **risk vs reward**
- **Survival and extraction** are the real win conditions
- Information (map knowledge, audio, ammo types) is your strongest weapon

…you will start to see steady progress.

Take it slow, use your SCAV wisely, and don’t be afraid to extract early.

Good luck in Tarkov — may your extractions be safe and your stashes full.
